Hey everyone — thanks for taking a look at this. We’re a boutique hotel group and we’ve outgrown our current booking/PMS setup and want something custom built that actually fits how we operate. Below is basically everything we’re thinking for the platform. Some of it’s firmed up, some of it is still rough, so use your judgment and flag anything that doesn’t make sense. We’re calling it LumenStay internally for now (marketing might rename it “GlowStay” later, TBD, don’t worry about that).
We need this live for six of our properties by the start of ski season this year, and honestly if you can do it faster that’s even better. We know that’s tight but we’ve heard other groups spin up similar systems fast so we’re hoping a good team can make it work.
1. Company Background
Lumen Hospitality Group operates six boutique hotels across two states (Colorado and Utah), each with its own personality/branding but shared back-of-house operations. We currently use a patchwork of a legacy PMS (property management system), a separate booking widget embedded on our Squarespace sites, a spreadsheet for housekeeping assignments, and a lot of manual phone/email coordination between front desk and revenue management.
1.1 Our properties (for context)
We figured it’d help to give you a quick rundown of the six properties, since they’re pretty different from each other and that probably matters for how flexible the system needs to be:
The Birchwood (Aspen, CO) — 42 rooms, our original property, heavy ski-season demand, does a lot of destination weddings in summer/fall. Runs on our oldest PMS install.
Copperline Inn (Breckenridge, CO) — 28 rooms, very seasonal (ski + summer mountain biking), smallest front-desk team (2-3 people on a shift).
The Wren House (Telluride, CO) — 19 rooms, our “quiet luxury” property, high ADR, low volume, guests expect a lot of white-glove service.
Sundowner Lodge (Park City, UT) — 51 rooms, our biggest property, closest thing we have to a “normal” hotel operation, does corporate/conference business in shoulder season.
Cedar & Salt (Moab, UT) — 24 rooms, desert/adventure-tourism guests, very different seasonality than the ski properties (busiest spring/fall, slow in winter).
The Ledger (Salt Lake City, UT) — 33 rooms, our only true “urban” property, mostly business travelers and airport layovers, least brand-differentiated of the six.
We mention all this because we suspect a system tuned only for ski-resort seasonality won’t work great for Moab or Salt Lake, and we want whoever builds this to actually think about that rather than assume one seasonal pattern fits everyone.
1.2 Why we’re doing this now
Our current legacy PMS vendor told us in the spring that they’re sunsetting the product we’re on (some kind of platform migration on their end) and we have “about a year” before we’re forced onto their new system, which we’ve looked at and don’t love — it’s expensive per-property and still doesn’t solve our multi-property visibility problem. That’s part of why the timeline in §7 feels urgent to us, even though we recognize it might not be a realistic engineering timeline. We’d rather hear the truth about what’s achievable than get a timeline that sounds good and then slips.
We want one platform that our front desk staff, housekeeping, revenue managers, and eventually our guests can all use. We’re a small but ambitious group and we think there’s a real opportunity to build something that could eventually be licensed to other boutique hotel groups too — so build it with that kind of extensibility in mind, though that’s not the primary goal right now.
Our current tech stack knowledge internally is limited — we don’t have an in-house engineering team, just a part-time IT contractor who manages our Wi-Fi and POS terminals. Whoever we pick will essentially be our engineering department for this project.
2. What We’re Trying to Build
At a high level:
A reservation and booking engine that replaces our current widget and connects to a central inventory across all properties.
A property-management core: room status, housekeeping, maintenance tickets, guest profiles.
A revenue management piece: dynamic pricing suggestions, competitor rate tracking, and channel management to OTAs (Expedia, Booking.com, Airbnb).
A guest-facing mobile app for check-in, digital key, and requesting services (extra towels, late checkout, etc.) — actually, on second thought, we might not need a native app if a really good mobile web experience can do all of this; we’ll leave that to your recommendation, but assume the guest experience must feel like a native app either way.
Loyalty and repeat-guest perks, ideally something guests would find comparable to what they get from Marriott Bonvoy or Hilton Honors, so they feel like staying with us is just as rewarding.
A staff-facing operations app for housekeeping and maintenance, usable on shared tablets at each property.
Basic reporting for ownership — occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, that kind of thing.
We don’t need this to be a “platform for the whole hotel industry” — Marriott/Hilton scale is not what we’re going for — but we do want it built properly, not duct-taped together, since we’re planning to grow.
2.1 A few things we go back and forth on
We want to be upfront that we haven’t fully made up our minds on a few things, and we’d rather tell you that than pretend we have a fixed spec:
Whether the loyalty program (§3.6) is a phase-1 must-have or something we could soft-launch a few months after everything else. Marcus (CEO) thinks it’s essential for differentiation; our revenue manager thinks it’s a distraction from getting the booking engine and channel manager right first.
Whether we build our own digital-key/mobile-check-in flow now or wait until we’ve standardized on one lock vendor across all six properties (currently split between Salto and Assa Abloy, see §4). Ripping out locks at three properties is its own project and budget we haven’t started.
Whether the “staff operations app” (§3.7) needs to be a separate installable app or can just be a responsive web view on the shared tablets. We lean toward whatever is cheaper/faster to build well.
How much of the revenue-management / dynamic-pricing piece (§3.4) needs to exist on day one versus whether we keep doing pricing manually in a spreadsheet for a while longer and just feed final rates into the new system. Our revenue manager has opinions here that we haven’t fully captured in this document.
We’re telling you this so you don’t assume we have more internal alignment than we do. Part of what we’re hoping a good vendor can do is help us make some of these calls with real tradeoffs in front of us, not just build whatever we describe most confidently.
3. Functional Areas
3.1 Reservations & Booking Engine
Direct booking widget embeddable on our existing Squarespace sites (and eventually a standalone booking site).
Real-time room-type availability across all properties from one inventory.
Rate plans: standard, non-refundable, member rate (see loyalty, §3.6), and property-specific promo codes.
Multi-room, multi-night booking with a cart-like flow.
Guest account creation at time of booking (optional — allow guest checkout too).
Booking modification and cancellation self-service, subject to rate-plan rules.
Group booking / block booking for weddings and events (at least 2 of our properties do a lot of wedding business).
3.2 Property Management Core
Room inventory per property: room types, room numbers, floor/building, features (view, accessibility, pet-friendly).
Reservation-to-room assignment, with manual override by front desk.
Room status board: clean/dirty/inspected/out-of-order, updated by housekeeping in real time.
Guest profile: stay history across all properties, preferences, notes, ID/passport capture for check-in compliance.
Front-desk check-in/check-out flow, including incidental charges (minibar, parking, resort fee).
Folio management and basic billing; we’ll keep our existing payment processor (see §4) rather than build new payment rails.
3.3 Housekeeping & Maintenance
Daily housekeeping assignment board, drag-and-drop by room, assignable to specific staff.
Mobile-friendly status updates from housekeeping staff (clean/dirty/inspected) from a shared tablet or their own phone.
Maintenance ticket creation (by any staff role) with photo attachment, priority, and assignment.
Preventive maintenance scheduling (HVAC filters, fire extinguisher checks, etc.) — this can be basic to start.
3.3a A bit more on maintenance, since we glossed over it
Thinking about this more, maintenance is actually a bigger deal for us operationally than the earlier bullet points suggest. Our properties range from a historic building (The Birchwood, built in the 1920s and renovated twice) to a much newer build (Sundowner Lodge). The older buildings generate a lot more maintenance tickets, and there’s institutional knowledge among long-tenured maintenance staff about quirks in specific rooms (room 214 at The Birchwood has a radiator that needs to be bled every few weeks, that kind of thing) that currently lives entirely in people’s heads. We’d love for the system to eventually be a place where that kind of tribal knowledge gets captured against specific rooms/assets, even if that’s a phase 2 or phase 3 idea rather than something for initial launch.
3.4 Revenue Management
Dynamic pricing suggestions based on occupancy pace, day-of-week, and historical demand.
Competitor rate visibility (we’ll want to discuss how — we don’t currently have a rate-shopping tool, open to your recommendation).
Channel manager to push rates/availability to OTAs (Expedia, Booking.com, Airbnb, and Google Hotel Ads) and pull back reservations from them into the same inventory.
Reporting: occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, pace-vs-last-year, by property and consolidated.
3.5 Guest Mobile Experience
Mobile check-in (skip the front desk) with ID verification.
Digital room key (this might require hardware — we’re open to your recommendation on lock vendors; we currently have a mix of two different lock brands across properties, sorry).
In-stay service requests: housekeeping requests, maintenance issues, concierge questions, late checkout.
Push notifications for offers, local recommendations, and pre-arrival messaging.
We want this to feel “as good as” what guests get from major hotel brand apps.
3.6 Loyalty Program
Point accrual per stay, redeemable for free nights or upgrades.
Tiering (e.g., silver/gold/platinum) with perks like early check-in, late checkout, welcome amenity.
Member-only rate visible in the booking engine once logged in.
We’d like this to feel comparable to major hotel loyalty programs so guests don’t feel like they’re settling for less by staying with us instead of a big chain. We are not looking to reproduce anyone else’s program exactly — just build something in that spirit that’s ours.
3.7 Staff Operations App
Shared-tablet-friendly interface for housekeeping and maintenance at each property.
Role-based views: housekeeping sees their assignment board; maintenance sees ticket queue; front desk sees reservations/room status; general manager sees everything for their property; ownership sees consolidated view across all properties.
3.7a A note on roles and who does what
Since we don’t have a formal org chart written down anywhere, here’s roughly who’s who across the group, in case it helps you think about the staff-facing side of things:
General Managers (one per property) — day-to-day operations, staffing, guest escalations. Comfortable with tech but not power users of anything complicated.
Front desk agents — check-in/out, folio questions, phone reservations. High turnover role, so whatever they use needs to be learnable in a single shift of training.
Housekeeping supervisors and staff — room status updates, assignment boards. At three properties, several housekeeping staff are more comfortable in Spanish than English, so some amount of bilingual support in the interface would be appreciated, though we haven’t scoped exactly what that means.
Maintenance/engineering staff — shared across properties in some cases (e.g., one maintenance lead covers both Copperline and The Wren House since they’re close together).
Revenue manager — one person, covers all six properties, currently does pricing in a spreadsheet and manually updates OTA extranets by hand (yes, really).
Marcus (CEO/Founder) and a small ownership/finance team — want the consolidated reporting view, don’t need day-to-day operational access.
3.8 Reporting & Analytics
Ownership dashboard: occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, booking pace, channel mix, loyalty enrollment.
Exportable reports (CSV at minimum).
We’d love AI-generated summaries eventually — like a weekly digest that tells ownership what happened and why — but that can be a phase 2 thing, or phase 1 if it’s not too much extra effort.
